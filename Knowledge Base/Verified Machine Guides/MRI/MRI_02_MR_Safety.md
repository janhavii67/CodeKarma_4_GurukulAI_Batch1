# MRI safety: screening and controlled access

## Current reference set

ACR lists its 2026 Manual on MR Safety as available for MR practitioners, technologists, medical physicists, administrators, and other healthcare professionals. The manual and the site's current written MR-safety policies are the primary references for operational decisions; this summary does not replace them.

## Entry and device screening

The FDA states that screening people and objects entering the MR environment is critical because objects attracted by the magnet may become projectiles. The FDA also says a device with unknown MRI safety status should be assumed to be MR Unsafe. Device-specific MR labeling and conditions must be verified before entry; do not rely on appearance or an AI response to classify an implant or object.

## Access control and escalation

ACR provides current screening forms, safety resources, and the 2026 manual. The MRI site should use its controlled-access process and have MR-trained staff make screening and exception decisions. If a device, implant, foreign object, or person's screening status is unknown or uncertain, stop the entry decision and escalate to the responsible MR safety personnel.

## Exact-system boundary

This guide does not contain the installed scanner's field strength, spatial-field-gradient limits, RF limits, coil restrictions, implant conditions, or emergency procedures. Obtain those from the current device labeling, manufacturer documentation, ACR manual, and local MR-safety policy.
