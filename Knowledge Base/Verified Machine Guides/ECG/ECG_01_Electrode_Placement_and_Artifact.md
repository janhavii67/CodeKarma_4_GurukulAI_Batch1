# ECG recording quality: electrode placement and artifact awareness

## Electrode placement matters

The AHA scientific statement on hospital ECG monitoring emphasizes accurate electrode placement and appropriate lead selection. Placement practices for continuous monitoring differ from standard 12-lead ECG placement. Moving electrodes to the torso may be used for continuous monitoring, but it should not be mistaken for the standard limb-lead placement used for a diagnostic 12-lead recording.

## Artifact can look like a rhythm problem

Movement, poor electrode contact, and misplaced electrodes can create artifacts that affect recorded waveforms. The AHA describes cases where artifact and misplaced leads contributed to incorrect rhythm interpretation and unnecessary interventions. When a tracing looks inconsistent with the patient or surrounding context, verify the recording setup and escalate interpretation to the responsible clinician rather than asking an AI to diagnose it.

## Device and patient context

Use the lead configuration, electrode type, skin preparation, and acquisition steps in the facility's approved procedure and the exact ECG model's user manual. Do not copy settings or hookup diagrams from a different monitor model or care setting.

## Source age

This source is a 2017 AHA scientific statement. It is useful for the stated electrode-placement and artifact principles; the facility must use current policies and device instructions for operational practice.
