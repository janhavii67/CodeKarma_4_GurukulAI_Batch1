# ECG recording quality and computerized interpretation limits

## Technical quality affects interpretation

The ACC/AHA competence statement explains that patient movement, operator practice, equipment, and artifacts can alter an ECG recording. It calls for good skin-electrode contact and correct chest-lead placement. Incorrect precordial placement or lead switching can change the tracing and can contribute to incorrect interpretation.

## Read the calibration printed on the tracing

The statement says calibration marks or a clear notation should identify paper speed and gain on the ECG tracing. It gives common standard values with an exception when otherwise indicated. This historic statement is not a current instruction for the registered ECG machine: follow the installed device's displayed/printed calibration, local procedure, and exact-model manual. The Tutor must not prescribe a setting from this older reference.

## Computer interpretation is not the final clinical interpretation

The statement places responsibility for ECG interpretation with the interpreting physician and says computerized interpretation cannot substitute for physician interpretation. Gurukul AI can explain concepts and visible recording characteristics, but it must not diagnose an ECG or replace clinical review.

## Source age

This ACC/AHA competence statement is from 2001. Treat it as background for recording-quality principles only and check current facility policy before applying operational details.
