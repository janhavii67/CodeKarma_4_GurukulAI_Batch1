# CT scanner quality control: roles, checks, and escalation

## Scope

This is a short learning summary of ACR CT quality-control guidance. It is not the operating manual for the installed scanner. The local CT model is not registered in Gurukul AI, so scanner menus, test methods, action limits, and clinical settings must come from the facility's written procedure, the qualified medical physicist (QMP), and the manufacturer's instructions for that exact unit.

## Who owns the program

ACR says a CT quality-control program is established and implemented under QMP supervision. The facility should document acceptance testing, routine technologist QC, an annual medical-physicist system performance evaluation, preventive maintenance, and service work. An on-site radiologic technologist should be identified to perform the assigned routine checks using written methods.

## Routine checks described by ACR

The ACR page lists daily water CT-number and standard-deviation checks and daily artifact evaluation. It lists a visual checklist and display-monitor QC monthly, with laser-printer checks at the stated weekly or monthly interval when that printer type is used. The facility's own approved QC plan and current ACR manual determine the applicable method and limits. Do not infer a pass/fail result from an image or an AI summary.

## Results outside limits or after repair

If a QC result falls outside the facility's control limits, document it and involve the QMP for unresolved problems. ACR assigns preventive maintenance and corrective service to qualified service engineers. The QMP determines what evaluation is required after a major component repair or replacement; technologist QC is required before clinical use in the cases specified by ACR. Do not bypass a hold or return-to-service decision.
