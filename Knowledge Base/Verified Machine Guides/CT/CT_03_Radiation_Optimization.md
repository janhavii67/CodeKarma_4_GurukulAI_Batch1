# CT and x-ray imaging: justify and optimize exposure

## Justification and optimization

The FDA describes two related responsibilities: the referring provider justifies the examination, and the imaging team optimizes the examination. The aim is an image quality adequate for the clinical task using the lowest radiation dose reasonably necessary. Optimization depends on the clinical indication, patient size, anatomy, image-quality need, and capabilities of the installed system.

## Facility quality assurance

The FDA recommends facility protocols or technique charts developed for the clinical task and patient group, equipment maintenance and testing, staff training, and regular quality-control checks. Dose monitoring can identify unusual patterns for investigation and follow-up. Diagnostic reference levels are comparison tools for optimization; they are not dose limits or automatic pass/fail thresholds.

## CT dose indices

For CT, the FDA identifies CTDIvol and dose-length product (DLP) as standardized dose indices based on standardized phantom measurements. These indices describe aspects of scanner output and should be interpreted with the examination and patient context. The Tutor must not convert them into a patient-specific safety judgment.

## Local protocol boundary

This guide gives general FDA principles. It does not supply CT acquisition settings. Use the facility's approved protocol and the manufacturer's instructions for the exact scanner; refer protocol changes to the responsible radiologist, QMP, and technologist team.
