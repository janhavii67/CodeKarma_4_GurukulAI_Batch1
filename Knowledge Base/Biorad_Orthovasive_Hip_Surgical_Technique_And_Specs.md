# Biorad Orthovasive Hip Systems: Technical Specifications & Surgical Operative Guide

## 1. Implant Portfolio & Biomaterial Architecture

Orthovasive (a specialized division of Biorad Medisys Pvt. Ltd.) manufactures premier Total Hip Arthroplasty (THA) implant systems engineered for long-term biological osteointegration, maximum range of motion, and elimination of post-operative dislocations.

### A. SIGNATURE NX Uncemented Trabecular Acetabular Cup
* **Material**: Medical-grade Titanium alloy (**Ti6Al4V ELI** per ASTM F136).
* **Trabecular Porosity**: Engineered with an open interconnected 3D trabecular lattice structure providing **60% to 70% overall porosity** with a mean pore diameter of **650 µm**—precisely matching human cancellous bone morphology to accelerate osteoblast migration, angiogenesis, and long-term biological fixation.
* **Friction & Scratch Stability**: High initial coefficient of friction (µ > 0.85 against cancellous bone) guarantees immediate press-fit mechanical stability prior to bone ingrowth.
* **Sizing Options**: Diameters from **44 mm to 64 mm** in exact 2 mm incremental steps:
  * 44 mm, 46 mm, 48 mm, 50 mm, 52 mm, 54 mm, 56 mm, 58 mm, 60 mm, 62 mm, 64 mm.
* **Hole Configurations**:
  * **Cluster-Hole Dome**: 6 to 9 divergent screw holes localized to the superior-posterior quadrant (safe zone) for supplemental cancellous bone screw fixation in compromised bone.
  * **Multi-Hole Dome**: Designed for revision cases and dysplastic acetabular roofs requiring multiple divergent anchor screws.
* **Fixation Screws**: 6.5 mm self-tapping cancellous bone screws (lengths: 15 mm to 50 mm in 5 mm increments) with deep hex drives and low-profile spherical heads.

### B. SIGNATURE Duo (Dual Mobility Acetabular System)
* **Kinematic Principle**: Features two articulating interfaces:
  1. A small articulation between the 28 mm inner ceramic or metallic femoral head and a mobile polyethylene bearing liner.
  2. A large articulation between the outer spherical surface of the mobile polyethylene liner and the mirror-polished inner surface of the metal acetabular shell.
* **Clinical Purpose**: Drastically increases the effective jumping distance required for femoral head displacement, reducing post-operative dislocation rates to <0.5%.
* **Indications**:
  * High-risk primary THA (elderly patients, neuromuscular disorders, Parkinson's disease, spine-hip syndrome, revision arthroplasty, and abductor deficiency).

### C. SIGNATURE Cementless Hydroxyapatite (HA) Femoral Stem
* **Geometry**: Tapered flat-wedge rectangular cross-section providing high torsional stability and physiological proximal load transfer into the calcar and greater trochanter while avoiding distal diaphyseal stress shielding.
* **Surface Coating**: Dual-zone coating:
  * Proximal one-third: 150 µm vacuum plasma-sprayed **Hydroxyapatite (HA)** over corundum-blasted titanium substrate (crystallinity > 65%, Ca/P ratio 1.67).
  * Distal two-thirds: Micro-textured grit-blasted titanium for smooth distal fit without rigid distal fixation.
* **Neck Geometries**:
  * **Standard 130° Neck-Shaft Angle**: Anatomical reconstruction of standard hip center.
  * **Extended Lateralized 135° Neck-Shaft Angle**: Restores abductor lever arm without lengthening leg in wide femoral canal anatomy.
* **Taper**: Industry-standard **12/14 Euro Taper** with micro-grooved surface to prevent ceramic head fretting corrosion.
* **Stem Sizing**: Sizes 1 through 10 in proportional 1 mm cross-sectional progressions.

### D. SIGNATURE Polished Cemented Femoral Stem
* **Geometry**: Triple-tapered, collarless, polished Cobalt-Chromium-Molybdenum alloy (CoCrMo per ASTM F75) stem with rounded corners.
* **Fixation Concept**: French taper-slip principle allowing controlled subsidence within the PMMA cement mantle, converting shear forces into radial compressive forces.
* **Centralizers**: Radiopaque PMMA distal centralizer (diameter 10 mm to 18 mm) and proximal centralizer wings ensuring uniform 2 mm cement mantle around the stem.

### E. Bearing Couples (Ceramics & XLPE)
1. **BIOLOX® delta 4th Generation Ceramic Heads**:
   * Alumina Matrix Composite (75% Al2O3, 24% ZrO2, 1% Cr2O3 / SrO).
   * Diameters: 28 mm, 32 mm, 36 mm.
   * Neck Length Offsets: Short (-3.5 mm), Medium (0 mm), Long (+3.5 mm), and Extra-Long (+7.0 mm with skirt).
   * Burst strength > 46 kN; ultra-low wear rate (< 1 mg/million cycles against XLPE).
2. **XLPE Highly Cross-Linked Polyethylene Liners**:
   * Gamma irradiated (75–100 kGy) and remelted or Vitamin E stabilized medical-grade GUR 1020 UHMWPE.
   * Styles: Neutral Flat (maximum range of motion), 20° Posterior Hooded (anti-subluxation lip), and Lateralized Offset.
   * Anti-rotation star tabs lock positively into acetabular shell rim scallops.

## 2. Step-by-Step Surgical Operative Technique (THA)

### Phase 1: Patient Positioning & Surgical Approach
1. Position patient in lateral decubitus position on OR table using rigid pelvic positioner (anterior pubic and posterior sacral supports). Confirm pelvis is exactly perpendicular to the operating table.
2. Prepare and drape operative limb using sterile impervious U-drapes and stockinette.
3. Perform **Posterolateral Approach (Southern/Moore)**:
   * Skin incision: Centered 4 cm distal to tip of greater trochanter, curved gently posterior towards posterior superior iliac spine (total length 10–12 cm).
   * Incise tensor fasciae latae and split gluteus maximus fibers along lines of pull.
   * Place Charnley retractor. Expose short external rotators (piriformis, obturator internus, gemelli).
   * Tag piriformis and conjoint tendon with stay sutures and peel from trochanteric crest. Reflect capsule to expose femoral head and neck.

### Phase 2: Femoral Neck Resection & Head Dislocation
1. Dislocate hip posteriorly by bringing thigh into flexion, adduction, and internal rotation.
2. Mark femoral neck osteotomy using Femoral Resection Guide:
   * Level: Typically one finger-breadth (10–12 mm) above lesser trochanter along the calcar femorale.
   * Angle: 45° to the femoral shaft axis.
3. Resect femoral neck with oscillating saw. Measure diameter of excised femoral head using head caliper to verify baseline anatomy.

### Phase 3: Acetabular Preparation & Reaming
1. Place anterior retractor over anterior acetabular wall, inferior retractor under transverse acetabular ligament (TAL), and posterior retractor behind posterior wall.
2. Remove labrum, pulvinar fat pad, and osteophytes down to the bottom of the cotyloid fossa (true floor). Identify the Transverse Acetabular Ligament (TAL) as the key landmark for anteversion.
3. Begin spherical reaming with 42 mm acetabular reamer angled at **40°–45° abduction/inclination** and **15°–20° anteversion**.
4. Progressively upsize reamers in 1–2 mm increments until healthy bleeding subchondral bone is exposed circumferentially across anterior, posterior, and dome columns.
5. In hard bone, under-ream by 1 mm for press-fit (e.g., ream to 53 mm for a 54 mm cup). In osteoporotic bone, ream line-to-line.

### Phase 4: Acetabular Cup Impaction & Screw Fixation
1. Mount SIGNATURE NX Trabecular Cup onto Cup Positioner Handle.
2. Align handle with alignment rod (45° inclination, 15°–20° anteversion parallel to TAL plane).
3. Impact cup with mallet until tactile sensation and pitch change indicate complete bottoming out against medial wall. Verify absence of gap through dome observation hole.
4. If bone quality or primary stability requires supplementation, drill through safe-zone cluster holes with 3.2 mm drill bit through drill guide. Measure depth with depth gauge.
5. Insert 6.5 mm cancellous screws; drive home until heads are countersunk below the inner shell surface.
6. Clean cup cavity thoroughly; insert trial and then definitive XLPE liner. Tap home with spherical liner impactor, ensuring all locking tabs engage with audible clicks.

### Phase 5: Femoral Canal Broaching & Sizing
1. Position leg in figure-of-four position (flexion, internal rotation, adduction) with tibia vertical.
2. Place Hohmann retractors around proximal femur to elevate femoral neck.
3. Open femoral canal using Box Chisel / Canal Finder adjacent to the posterior margin of the greater trochanter, avoiding varus trajectory.
4. Begin broaching with Size 1 Broach on offset broach handle.
5. Progressively upsize broaches (Size 1, 2, 3...) keeping alignment parallel to posterior cortex.
6. Final broach size is reached when the broach achieves axial and rotational stability (resists rotational torque) and sits flush with the calcar osteotomy line.
7. Mill the calcar with Calcar Planer mounted on the broach post until osteotomy surface is smooth.

### Phase 6: Trial Reduction & Biomechanical Verification
1. Place Trial Neck (Standard 130° or Lateralized 135°) and Trial Femoral Head (e.g., 32 mm Medium) onto the seated broach.
2. Reduce hip by gentle traction, external rotation, and extension.
3. Conduct comprehensive biomechanical and stability testing:
   * **Shuck Test (Tension)**: Apply longitudinal axial traction on femur. Leg should distract 1–2 mm only (indicates proper abductor myofascial tension).
   * **Range of Motion**: Flex hip to 100° with 30° internal rotation (posterior dislocation test); extend hip with 30° external rotation and adduction (anterior impingement test). Confirm absence of impingement or subluxation.
   * **Leg Length & Offset**: Compare knee/heel positions with contralateral landmark. Adjust trial neck length (-3.5, 0, +3.5, +7) to match preoperative plan.

### Phase 7: Definitive Femoral Stem & Head Seating
1. Dislocate hip. Remove trial head and neck. Extract final broach.
2. Mount definitive SIGNATURE HA Stem onto stem inserter.
3. Align stem down the broached envelope; advance under light mallet taps until fully seated at the calcar line.
4. Thoroughly clean and dry the 12/14 Euro morse taper. Apply clean gauze to wipe away all moisture and blood.
5. Place definitive BIOLOX delta Ceramic Head onto taper. Twist 90° clockwise by hand to seat morse taper, then place plastic-tipped head impactor and strike with one firm axial mallet blow.
6. Clean joint space with pulsed lavage, reduce hip, reattach tagged short external rotators and capsule through drill holes in greater trochanter, place subfascial suction drain, and perform watertight layered anatomical closure.
