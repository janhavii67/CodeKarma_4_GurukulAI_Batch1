# Biorad Orthovasive Knee Systems: Technical Specifications & Surgical Operative Guide

## 1. Product Portfolio & Kinematic Architecture

Orthovasive (a specialized division of Biorad Medisys Pvt. Ltd.) manufactures premier joint arthroplasty systems engineered to restore natural knee kinematics, ensure joint stability, and accommodate high flexion for active lifestyles.

### A. GENUIN PS (Posterior Stabilized) Total Knee System
* **Kinematics**: Features a single-radius femoral curvature that maintains consistent ligament isometry and collateral tension from 0° to 110° flexion. A cam-and-spine mechanism engages at 60° flexion to replicate physiological femoral rollback and prevent anterior subluxation.
* **Maximum Flexion**: Safely supports active deep flexion up to **155°** with an optimized anterior trochlear groove that reduces patellofemoral contact pressure.
* **Tibial Locking Mechanism**: Precision dovetail slide-and-lock configuration paired with a central locking screw. Backside micro-motion is restricted to less than 5 µm to eliminate backside polyethylene wear and osteolysis.
* **Sizing Matrix**:
  * Femoral Components: 8 proportional anatomical sizes (Size 1 to Size 8), available in Left and Right geometries.
  * Tibial Trays: 7 universal symmetric sizes (Size 1 to Size 7) offering complete interchangeability across adjacent femoral sizes.
  * Polyethylene Inserts (XLPE): Thickness options in 9 mm, 10 mm, 11 mm, 12 mm, 14 mm, 16 mm, 18 mm, and 20 mm.
* **Regulatory Clearances**: US FDA 510(k) Cleared (K200765), CE 2195 Certified.

### B. GENIUS CR (Cruciate Retaining) Total Knee System
* **Indication**: Preserves the functional posterior cruciate ligament (PCL) in patients with intact ligamentous stability.
* **Articular Profile**: Low-constraint dished tibial geometry allowing physiological PCL rollback, internal-external rotation (up to 15°), and natural proprioceptive feedback.
* **Component Material**: Cast Cobalt-Chromium-Molybdenum alloy (CoCrMo per ASTM F75) with ultra-polished articular finish (Ra < 0.02 µm).

### C. OURO PS/CR (Titanium Niobium Nitride Gold Knee)
* **Surface Technology**: Multi-layer Physical Vapor Deposition (PVD) coating of **Titanium Niobium Nitride (TiNbN)** applied over high-strength CoCrMo substrate.
* **Clinical Benefits**:
  * **Hypoallergenic Shield**: Completely isolates base alloy elements (Nickel, Chromium, Cobalt), preventing allergic dermatitis, synovitis, or aseptic loosening in metal-sensitive patients (10–15% of the general population).
  * **Wear Reduction**: Ceramic-like surface hardness (>2200 Vickers HV vs ~400 HV for untreated CoCrMo) reduces wear particles by up to 65% on ISO 14243 wear simulator tests.
  * **Low Friction & Wettability**: Reduced water contact angle promotes superior synovial fluid lubrication film.
* **Certifications**: CE 2195 Marked.

### D. Excel MPK (Medial Pivot Knee System)
* **Biomechanics**: Based on natural knee joint motion where the medial femoral condyle functions as a high-conformity "ball-and-socket" pivot center, while the lateral condyle slides freely anteroposteriorly during flexion up to 155°.
* **Tibial Design**: 3° built-in posterior slope with an asymmetric keel designed to optimize load distribution across the proximal tibial cancellous bone bed.
* **Clinical Result**: Eliminates paradoxical anterior femoral slide ("mid-flexion instability"), resulting in a knee that feels more natural and stable during stair descent and deep squatting.

### E. Evocon Revision Knee System
* **Modularity**: Distal femoral augment blocks (5 mm, 10 mm, 15 mm), posterior femoral augments (5 mm, 10 mm), and tibial half/full wedges.
* **Stem Extensions**: Intramedullary fluted and smooth stems (diameters 10 mm to 24 mm in 2 mm increments; lengths 100 mm, 135 mm, 175 mm) in straight and offset geometries (2 mm, 4 mm, 6 mm offset).
* **Insert Constraints**: Constrained Condylar Knee (CCK) inserts with extended reinforced spine for severe ligamentous insufficiency or collateral ligament laxity.

## 2. Step-by-Step Surgical Operative Technique (TKA)

### Phase 1: Patient Positioning & Exposure
1. Position patient supine on OR table with a lateral thigh support and foot rest allowing effortless 0° extension to 120° flexion.
2. Apply pneumatic tourniquet to upper thigh (inflate to 250–300 mmHg after limb exsanguination with Esmarch bandage).
3. Perform standard midline anterior longitudinal skin incision (12–15 cm) centered over the patella.
4. Execute medial parapatellar arthrotomy: incise quadriceps tendon 5 mm medial to rectus femoris, carry incision along medial border of patella, and continue along medial margin of patellar tendon ending 1 cm distal to tibial tubercle.
5. Evert or subluxate patella laterally; excise hypertrophic synovium and anterior horn of medial/lateral menisci.

### Phase 2: Distal Femoral Resection
1. Drill an 8 mm intramedullary pilot hole in the distal femur 10 mm anterior to the PCL insertion point in the intercondylar notch.
2. Insert intramedullary femoral alignment rod fitted with the Distal Femoral Resection Guide.
3. Set the valgus angle (standard: 5° to 7° based on pre-operative long-leg standing scanogram).
4. Secure distal femoral cutting block with 3.2 mm headless fixation pins.
5. Resect distal femur using an oscillating bone saw (blade thickness 1.27 mm) with continuous cold saline irrigation to prevent thermal bone necrosis. Verify flat resection using angel wing/feelers.

### Phase 3: Proximal Tibial Resection
1. Assemble Extramedullary Tibial Alignment Guide. Align proximally with medial third of tibial tubercle; align distally with second metatarsal and 5–10 mm medial to anterior ankle center.
2. Adjust posterior slope to 3° (matching implant design). Set tibial stylus to resect 9 mm from the highest intact tibial condyle (or 2 mm from deepest eroded defect).
3. Pin the tibial cutting block securely with headless and headed cross-pins.
4. Execute proximal tibial resection with oscillating saw blade flush against the guide slot. Verify square planar surface with alignment rod and check-plate.

### Phase 4: Femoral Sizing & 4-in-1 Chamfer Resection
1. Place Femoral Sizing Guide flush against the resected distal femur with posterior feet contacting both posterior condyles.
2. Set external rotation (standard: 3° external rotation relative to posterior condylar axis to align parallel to transepicondylar line).
3. Read the indicated femoral size on the calibrated boom (sizes 1 to 8).
4. Secure the corresponding 4-in-1 Femoral Resection Block with pins.
5. Complete five cuts in sequence:
   * Anterior cut
   * Posterior cut
   * Anterior chamfer cut
   * Posterior chamfer cut
   * Intercondylar notch cut (for PS housing box using PS notch punch guide)

### Phase 5: Gap Balancing & Trial Reduction
1. Insert flexion and extension spacer blocks with tensioner. Ensure equal rectangular flexion and extension gaps (symmetric collateral tension in 0° extension and 90° flexion).
2. Insert Trial Femoral Component, Trial Tibial Baseplate, and Trial Articular Poly Insert.
3. Reduce joint and conduct full range of motion test:
   * Confirm passive full extension (0°) with no recurvatum.
   * Confirm deep flexion (>130°) without premature posterior impingement.
   * Check mediolateral stability: 1–2 mm laxity in mid-flexion is physiological.
   * Assess patellar tracking ("no thumb rule"): the trial patella must track centrally within the femoral sulcus without tilting or subluxation.

### Phase 6: Patellar Preparation (Resurfacing / Insetting)
1. Measure total patellar thickness using caliper (e.g., 22 mm).
2. Resect patella using patellar osteotomy clamp to remove anterior articular bone, ensuring 12–14 mm of residual bony composite.
3. Drill 3 fixation peg holes using Patellar Drill Template.
4. Prepare 3-peg biconvex all-polyethylene patellar trial (diameters: 29 mm, 32 mm, 35 mm, 38 mm).

### Phase 7: Pulsed Lavage, Cementation & Final Implant Seating
1. Thoroughly irrigate resected bony beds using High-Pressure Pulsed Lavage (saline 2–3 liters) to clear fat, marrow, and micro-debris for optimal cement interdigitation.
2. Dry surfaces meticulously with clean sponges and suction.
3. Prepare high-viscosity polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA) radiopaque bone cement with gentamicin. Mix in vacuum bowl to eliminate porosity.
4. Apply doughy cement (working phase at 3–4 minutes) to distal/posterior femur, femoral implant pockets, and proximal tibial bone bed.
5. Seat Tibial Baseplate using Tibial Impactor; tap firmly until fully seated.
6. Seat Femoral Component using Femoral Impactor, driving along anterior-posterior axis.
7. Insert trial insert and bring knee into 10°–20° extension under axial compression to pressurize cement mantle uniformly.
8. Meticulously curette all extruded cement flash from posterior condyles, gutters, and periphery before polymerization.
9. Insert definitive XLPE insert; advance into dovetail mechanism and secure with locking screw tightened to recommended torque.
10. Final irrigation, intra-articular cocktail injection (ropivacaine, ketorolac, epinephrine, tranexamic acid), drain placement (optional), and layered watertight closure.
